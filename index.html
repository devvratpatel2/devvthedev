01Interstitial Upsell System

181,000

New subscribers a month, up from about 11,000.

10%

conversion, up from 0.3%

7+

microservices in the path, one architecture

Result: subscriber acquisition grew from about 11,000 to 181,000 a month, conversion rose from 0.3% to 10%, across 7 or more microservices.

The decision
I led the architecture and delivery across 7+ microservices. We wrote up three architecture options and I chose bounded orchestration: the upsell flow is coordinated inside one clear boundary instead of being spread across every service it touches.
The tradeoff
Each option traded technical debt against delivery timeline. The bounded approach could ship on schedule without adding new coupling to all seven services.
What it changed
Conversion went from 0.3% to 10%. The eligibility integration and experimentation setup mean every change since ships behind an A/B test, so it comes with a number.

02Enhanced Grocery Subscription Landing Page

One customer flow, delivered by three teams that each owned a different piece of the page.

Different widgets Different frameworks One set of integration contracts

The decision
I designed the end-to-end architecture and turned the customer flow into explicit integration contracts between the teams, so each one could build its widgets on its own framework without waiting on the others.
The tradeoff
Keeping each team on its own framework avoided a rewrite and kept everyone moving in parallel. The cost was more upfront work pinning down exactly what crosses each seam.
What it changed
The contracts became the shared reference for the page, and funnel views made experiment results readable widget by widget, so the page keeps getting iterated on instead of frozen at launch.

03FTC Double Opt-In Compliance

Shipped against a fixed legal deadline, with the rule built in so it can't be quietly undone.

The requirement lives in the service contracts and the tests. A future change that skips it fails before it reaches a customer.

The decision
I owned the system design and led two engineers through implementation, working as an away-team contributor in another team's services.
The tradeoff
The date couldn't move, so scope stayed narrow. The effort went into making the rule hard to break rather than into extras.
What it changed
Compliance stopped depending on people remembering it. It's enforced by contracts and tests that run on every change.

04Amazon Physical Location Finder

Before changing anything I traced how the system really worked: its dependencies, its auth, its deployment. Then I led the redesign on that map.
